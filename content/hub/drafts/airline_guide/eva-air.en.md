# Flying with a Dog on EVA Air

## Overview

EVA Air is Taiwan's second-largest international airline and a member of the **Star Alliance**. From its main hub at **Taipei Taoyuan International Airport (TPE)**, EVA Air serves destinations throughout **Asia, Europe, North America and Oceania**.

EVA Air accepts companion dogs on most international routes. Depending on the destination and operational constraints, pets generally travel **in the aircraft's climate-controlled hold**. Companion pets are **not normally accepted in the passenger cabin**, except for recognised assistance dogs.

As with all international travel, transporting a dog depends on both the airline's policy and the veterinary import requirements of the destination country.

# Cabin, hold or cargo

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_eva_air | cabin_policy}}

Companion dogs are generally **not accepted in the passenger cabin**. Only recognised assistance dogs travelling with eligible passengers may remain in the cabin in accordance with applicable regulations.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_eva_air | hold_policy}}

Most companion dogs travel inside the aircraft's ventilated, pressurised and temperature-controlled hold. Requirements generally include advance reservation; an IATA-compliant rigid kennel; valid veterinary documentation; and acceptance depending on aircraft type and route. Some destinations may also require cargo transport instead of checked baggage.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_eva_air | breed_restrictions}}

Certain brachycephalic breeds may be subject to additional operational restrictions because of increased respiratory risks. Seasonal temperature restrictions may also apply.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify destination-country import requirements;
- ensure your dog's vaccinations are up to date;
- reserve your dog's transport with EVA Air;
- prepare an IATA-compliant kennel if required.

Some countries require import permits, health certificates or quarantine procedures in addition to airline approval.

# Common mistakes

## Assuming cabin transport is available

Most companion dogs travel in the hold.

## Using a kennel that does not comply with IATA standards

Only compliant kennels are accepted.

## Forgetting destination-country regulations

Airline approval never replaces veterinary or customs requirements.

## Waiting too long before booking

Pet capacity is limited on every flight.

# Expert advice

EVA Air is an excellent choice for long-haul travel with a dog between Asia, Europe and North America. For complex international itineraries, begin preparing veterinary documentation several weeks—or even months—before departure, depending on your destination.
