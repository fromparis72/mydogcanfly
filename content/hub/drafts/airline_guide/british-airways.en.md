# Flying with a Dog on British Airways

## Overview

British Airways is the United Kingdom's flag carrier and a founding member of the **oneworld Alliance**. Operating primarily from **London Heathrow (LHR)** and **London Gatwick (LGW)**, it serves more than 200 destinations across Europe, North America, Africa, Asia, the Middle East and Oceania.

For travellers flying with a dog, British Airways is **very different from most European airlines**. With the exception of recognised **assistance dogs**, companion animals are **not accepted in the passenger cabin or as checked baggage**. Instead, they normally travel through **IAG Cargo**, the specialist animal transport division serving British Airways and several other airlines. This policy is largely influenced by the United Kingdom's animal import regulations.

A successful journey depends on three independent elements:

- British Airways' transport policy;
- destination-country veterinary and customs requirements;
- operational constraints such as aircraft type, routing, weather and cargo availability.

# Is British Airways a good choice?

Yes—but only if you understand its specific model. Unlike most European airlines, British Airways has chosen to centralise companion animal transport through **IAG Cargo**, providing specialised handling from departure to arrival. Its main strengths include professional live-animal handling, a worldwide long-haul network, strong expertise for international relocations, and dedicated cargo facilities at Heathrow. For owners of small dogs hoping to travel in the cabin, however, British Airways is generally **not the right choice**.

# Cabin, cargo or assistance dogs

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_british_airways | cabin_policy}}

Companion dogs are **not accepted in the passenger cabin**. The only exception concerns recognised assistance dogs travelling with eligible passengers on approved routes and subject to documentation requirements.

Cargo transport: {{fait: airline_british_airways | hold_policy}}

Most dogs travel through **IAG Cargo's Pet Transport service**. Animals are transported in IATA-compliant kennels and handled by dedicated live-animal teams throughout the journey. Depending on the route, bookings are made either directly with IAG Cargo or through an approved pet relocation company.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_british_airways | breed_restrictions}}

Certain brachycephalic breeds and breeds subject to national legislation may face additional transport restrictions. Destination-country regulations may also prohibit entry for specific breeds.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify UK or destination-country import rules;
- ensure your dog's microchip and vaccinations are compliant;
- contact IAG Cargo or an approved pet shipper;
- prepare an IATA-compliant kennel.

Unlike traditional airlines, booking your own passenger ticket does **not** automatically reserve transport for your dog.

# Connecting through London

London Heathrow is one of the world's busiest international cargo hubs. Whenever possible, avoid unnecessary transfers, verify that every flight is operated by British Airways or another airline accepting live animals, and allow sufficient connection time for cargo processing.

# Common mistakes

## Assuming pets can travel in the cabin

They cannot, except recognised assistance dogs.

## Booking your own ticket without arranging cargo

Passenger and pet reservations are handled separately.

## Forgetting UK import regulations

The United Kingdom has specific animal health requirements that differ from many EU countries.

## Using a non-IATA kennel

IAG Cargo requires compliant transport containers.

# Expert advice

British Airways is an excellent airline for **international pet relocation**, but it is not designed for typical holiday travel with a small dog. If your goal is to keep your dog with you in the cabin, airlines such as Air France, Lufthansa or KLM will generally be more suitable. If you are relocating internationally with a large dog, however, British Airways and IAG Cargo are among the world's leading specialist operators.
