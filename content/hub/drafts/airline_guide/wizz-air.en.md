# Flying with a Dog on Wizz Air

## Overview

Wizz Air is one of Europe's fastest-growing ultra-low-cost airlines. From its bases across Central and Eastern Europe—including **Budapest, Bucharest, Warsaw, Vienna, Abu Dhabi, Rome and London Luton**—it serves more than 190 destinations throughout Europe, the Middle East and parts of North Africa.

For dog owners, Wizz Air applies a policy similar to that of Ryanair and easyJet. **Companion dogs are not accepted**, either in the passenger cabin or in the aircraft hold.

The only exception concerns **recognised assistance dogs**, which may travel free of charge on eligible flights, subject to advance notification and the required documentation.

If you are travelling with a pet dog, you will almost certainly need to choose another airline. MyDogCanFly automatically identifies these situations and recommends airlines serving the same route that accept companion dogs.

# Is Wizz Air a good choice?

For owners of companion dogs, **no**. Wizz Air does not transport pets as part of its commercial model. If your dog is not a recognised assistance dog, MyDogCanFly will generally recommend another airline operating the same route.

# Companion dogs

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_wizz_air | cabin_policy}}

Companion dogs are **not accepted in the passenger cabin**.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_wizz_air | hold_policy}}

Wizz Air **does not transport companion dogs in the aircraft hold**.

Assistance dogs: recognised assistance dogs may travel free of charge on eligible Wizz Air routes. Passengers must notify the airline before departure and provide all required documentation. Depending on the destination, additional health documentation may also be required.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify whether your dog qualifies as a recognised assistance dog;
- check destination-country import requirements;
- if travelling with a companion dog, choose another airline.

Booking first and checking the pet policy afterwards usually results in having to change your itinerary.

# Common mistakes

## Assuming all airlines transport pets

Many European low-cost airlines—including Wizz Air—do not.

## Confusing assistance dogs with emotional support animals

Only recognised assistance dogs are accepted.

## Booking before checking the airline's policy

Companion dogs cannot be added to an existing booking.

## Forgetting destination-country regulations

Even recognised assistance dogs must comply with veterinary import requirements.
