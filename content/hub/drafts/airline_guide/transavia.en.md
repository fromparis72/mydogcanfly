# Flying with a Dog on Transavia

## Overview

Transavia is the low-cost airline of the Air France–KLM Group. Operating mainly from **Amsterdam Schiphol (AMS), Rotterdam-The Hague (RTM), Eindhoven (EIN), Paris-Orly (ORY), Nantes (NTE), Lyon (LYS)** and several other European airports, it serves more than 100 destinations across Europe, North Africa and the Mediterranean.

Unlike many low-cost airlines, **Transavia accepts companion dogs**. Depending on their size, pets may travel **in the passenger cabin** or **in the aircraft's climate-controlled hold**. This makes Transavia one of the few European low-cost airlines genuinely suitable for travelling with a dog.

A successful journey depends on three independent factors:

- Transavia's pet transport policy;
- destination-country veterinary and customs requirements;
- operational constraints such as aircraft type, weather, routing and available pet capacity.

# Is Transavia a good choice?

Absolutely. Unlike many low-cost competitors, Transavia offers both **cabin** and **hold** transport for companion dogs. Its main strengths include pets accepted in cabin and hold, affordable pet transport fees, an excellent European leisure network, Air France–KLM operational standards, and a simple booking process. For many travellers departing France or the Netherlands, Transavia is one of the easiest low-cost airlines for flying with a dog.

# Cabin, hold or cargo

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_transavia | cabin_policy}}

Small dogs may travel in the passenger cabin inside an approved carrier placed beneath the seat. Cabin transport is subject to a maximum combined weight, carrier dimensions, and limited pet capacity. Pets must remain inside the carrier throughout the flight.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_transavia | hold_policy}}

Dogs exceeding cabin limits may travel inside the aircraft's ventilated and temperature-controlled hold. The kennel must comply with **IATA Live Animals Regulations** and allow the dog to stand, turn around and lie comfortably. Acceptance depends on aircraft type and destination.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_transavia | breed_restrictions}}

Certain brachycephalic breeds and breeds presenting increased transport risks may be subject to operational restrictions. Always verify eligibility before booking.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify destination-country regulations;
- ensure your dog's vaccinations are up to date;
- choose an IATA-compliant carrier or kennel;
- reserve your dog's place with Transavia.

Pet transport is subject to availability on every flight.

# Connecting flights

Most Transavia flights are operated as point-to-point services rather than complex hub connections. If your itinerary includes another airline, verify each carrier's pet policy and ensure your dog's transport has been confirmed for every flight.

# Common mistakes

## Waiting too long before reserving your dog

Only a limited number of pets are accepted on each aircraft.

## Using a non-compliant kennel

Hold transport requires an IATA-compliant kennel.

## Forgetting destination-country regulations

Airline approval never replaces veterinary or customs requirements.

## Assuming all low-cost airlines accept pets

Most do not. Transavia is one of the notable exceptions.

# Expert advice

Transavia is one of the best low-cost airlines in Europe for travelling with a dog. Its combination of affordable fares, cabin and hold transport, and the operational experience of the Air France–KLM Group makes it an excellent choice for holidays throughout Europe and the Mediterranean.
