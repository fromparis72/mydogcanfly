# Flying with a Dog on Delta Air Lines

## Overview

Delta Air Lines is one of the largest airlines in the United States and a founding member of the SkyTeam alliance. From its major hubs in **Atlanta (ATL), Detroit (DTW), Minneapolis–Saint Paul (MSP), Salt Lake City (SLC), New York (JFK), Los Angeles (LAX), Seattle (SEA)** and **Boston (BOS)**, Delta serves hundreds of destinations across North America, Europe, Latin America, Asia and Oceania.

For dog owners, Delta's policy differs from many European airlines. **Small dogs may travel in the passenger cabin on eligible flights**, while the former accompanied checked-pet service has been largely discontinued. Most larger dogs now travel through **Delta Cargo**, with exceptions for certain U.S. military and government personnel under specific conditions. International regulations and destination-country requirements also determine whether cargo transport is mandatory.

A successful journey depends on three independent factors:

- Delta's transport policy;
- destination-country veterinary and customs regulations;
- operational constraints such as aircraft type, weather, routing and pet capacity.

# Is Delta Air Lines a good choice?

Yes—provided your dog meets Delta's eligibility requirements. Delta is particularly attractive for owners of small dogs thanks to its vast domestic network and international partnerships. Its main strengths include an extensive U.S. network, strong international connectivity, small pets accepted in the passenger cabin, and clear online guidance for international pet travel. Owners of large dogs should carefully review Delta Cargo requirements before booking.

# Cabin, hold or cargo

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_delta | cabin_policy}}

Small dogs may travel inside the passenger cabin in an airline-approved carrier that fits completely beneath the seat. Only a limited number of pets are accepted in each cabin, so reservations should be made as early as possible. Pets must remain inside their carrier throughout boarding and the flight.

Cargo transport: {{fait: airline_delta | hold_policy}}

Unlike many European airlines, Delta generally **does not offer accompanied checked-pet transport for large companion animals**. Most larger pets travel through **Delta Cargo**, and this service is currently restricted primarily to active U.S. military personnel and U.S. Foreign Service Officers relocating under official orders. Some international destinations also require cargo transport regardless of the airline's own policy.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_delta | breed_restrictions}}

Breed restrictions may apply depending on destination-country regulations and operational safety requirements. Always verify eligibility before purchasing your ticket.

# Before booking

Before confirming your reservation:

- verify destination-country import regulations;
- ensure your dog is microchipped where required;
- prepare all veterinary documentation;
- reserve your pet with Delta.

International travel may require additional CDC documentation, import permits or health certificates depending on your destination.

# Connecting through the United States

If your itinerary includes connections, verify every operating airline's pet policy, allow sufficient transfer time, and remember that U.S. import rules for dogs have become significantly stricter in recent years. Codeshare flights may apply different pet rules than Delta-operated flights.

# Common mistakes

## Waiting too long before reserving cabin space

Pet capacity is limited on every aircraft.

## Assuming large dogs can travel as checked baggage

For most passengers, Delta now requires cargo transport for larger animals.

## Forgetting U.S. import regulations

CDC requirements may apply even when returning to the United States.

## Booking codeshare flights without checking

Partner airlines may have completely different pet policies.

# Expert advice

Delta is an excellent airline for travelling with a **small dog in the cabin**, particularly within North America. For owners of large dogs, planning requires much more preparation because Delta's traditional checked-pet service has been significantly reduced in favour of specialised cargo transport. If your itinerary includes international travel, always verify both Delta's policy and your destination country's import regulations before booking.
