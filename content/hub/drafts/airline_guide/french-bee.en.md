# Flying with a Dog on French Bee

## Overview

French Bee is a French long-haul low-cost airline operating from **Paris-Orly Airport (ORY)**. Its network includes destinations such as **Réunion Island, Tahiti, San Francisco, Los Angeles, Miami and New York**.

Unlike many low-cost airlines, **French Bee accepts companion dogs**. Depending on your dog's size, pets may travel **in the passenger cabin** or **in the aircraft's climate-controlled hold**. Animals exceeding operational limits must be transported as cargo. French Bee therefore offers one of the most attractive long-haul pet policies among European low-cost airlines.

A successful journey depends on three independent factors:

- French Bee's pet transport policy;
- destination-country veterinary and customs requirements;
- operational constraints such as aircraft type, weather, routing and available pet capacity.

# Cabin, hold or cargo

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_french_bee | cabin_policy}}

Small dogs may travel in the passenger cabin inside an approved carrier placed beneath the seat. Current policy includes a maximum combined weight (pet + carrier) of **8 kg**; one pet per passenger; advance reservation required; and pets must remain inside the carrier throughout the flight.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_french_bee | hold_policy}}

Dogs exceeding cabin limits may travel inside the aircraft's ventilated and temperature-controlled hold. Requirements include an IATA-compliant rigid kennel; advance reservation; transport subject to aircraft limitations; and veterinary documentation required. Animals exceeding operational limits may have to travel as cargo.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_french_bee | breed_restrictions}}

Certain brachycephalic breeds may be refused in the hold because of increased respiratory risks. French Category 1 dogs are prohibited in accordance with French legislation. Always verify destination-country import rules before booking.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify destination-country veterinary regulations;
- ensure your dog is microchipped and vaccinated;
- reserve pet transport as early as possible;
- prepare an IATA-compliant kennel if travelling in the hold.

Flights to the United States and French Polynesia may require additional documentation.

# Common mistakes

## Waiting too long before reserving your dog

Pet capacity is limited on every flight.

## Choosing a non-compliant kennel

Hold transport requires a kennel fully compliant with IATA standards.

## Forgetting destination-specific regulations

French Polynesia, Réunion and the United States all have different import requirements.

## Travelling with a brachycephalic breed

Some snub-nosed breeds may not be accepted in the hold.
