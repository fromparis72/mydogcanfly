# Flying with a Dog on Ryanair

## Overview

Ryanair is Europe's largest low-cost airline, operating more than 3,000 daily flights across over 35 countries. With major bases in **Dublin, London Stansted, Milan Bergamo, Madrid, Barcelona, Brussels Charleroi and Rome**, it serves one of the most extensive route networks in Europe.

For dog owners, Ryanair has one of the strictest pet policies in the industry. **Companion dogs are not accepted**, either in the passenger cabin or in the aircraft hold.

The only exception concerns **recognised assistance dogs**, which may travel free of charge on eligible routes, subject to advance approval and documentation. Assistance dogs are **not accepted** on flights to or from **Morocco** and **Israel**. Emotional support animals are not recognised as assistance dogs.

If you are travelling with a pet dog, you will almost always need to choose another airline. MyDogCanFly automatically detects these situations and recommends airlines serving the same route that actually accept dogs.

# Is Ryanair a good choice?

For travellers with a companion dog, **no**. Ryanair's ultra-low-cost business model does not include transporting pets. If your dog is not an officially recognised assistance dog, MyDogCanFly will usually recommend another airline operating the same route.

# Companion dogs

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_ryanair | cabin_policy}}

Companion dogs are **not accepted in the passenger cabin**.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_ryanair | hold_policy}}

Ryanair **does not transport companion dogs in the aircraft hold**.

Assistance dogs: recognised assistance dogs may travel free of charge on eligible Ryanair flights. To qualify, the dog must be trained by an organisation recognised by **ADI**, **Assistance Dogs UK** or **IGDF**, must wear an identifying harness, and must be pre-notified to Ryanair. A maximum of four assistance dogs are accepted per flight. Emotional support animals are not accepted.

# Before booking

Before purchasing a Ryanair ticket:

- verify whether your dog qualifies as a recognised assistance dog;
- check destination-country animal import rules;
- if travelling with a companion dog, select another airline from the start.

Buying the ticket first and looking for pet transport afterwards usually results in having to change your travel plans.

# Common mistakes

## Assuming every airline accepts pets

Many European low-cost airlines—including Ryanair—do not transport companion animals.

## Confusing assistance dogs with emotional support animals

Only officially recognised assistance dogs are accepted.

## Forgetting route restrictions

Assistance dogs are not accepted on Ryanair flights to or from Morocco and Israel.

## Not notifying Ryanair in advance

Failure to pre-advise the airline may result in the assistance service not being available.
