# Flying with a Dog on Air Caraïbes

## Overview

Air Caraïbes is a French airline specializing in flights between **mainland France**, the **French Caribbean**, **French Guiana**, and selected destinations in North America. Operating primarily from **Paris-Orly Airport (ORY)**, it serves destinations including **Guadeloupe, Martinique, Saint Martin, French Guiana, Punta Cana, Cancún** and **Cayenne**.

For travellers flying with a dog, Air Caraïbes offers one of the most comprehensive pet policies among French leisure airlines. Depending on your dog's size, pets may travel **in the passenger cabin** or **in the aircraft's climate-controlled hold**. Animals exceeding operational limits must travel via cargo.

A successful journey depends on three independent factors:

- Air Caraïbes' pet transport policy;
- destination-country veterinary and customs requirements;
- operational constraints such as aircraft type, weather and available pet capacity.

# Is Air Caraïbes a good choice?

Yes. Air Caraïbes is one of the best airlines for travellers flying with a dog to the French Caribbean. Its main strengths include pets accepted in both cabin and hold, an extensive Caribbean network, experience with long-haul leisure travel, and clear pet transport procedures. For travellers heading to Martinique, Guadeloupe or French Guiana, Air Caraïbes is one of the leading choices.

# Cabin, hold or cargo

Cabin transport: {{fait: airline_air_caraibes | cabin_policy}}

Small dogs may travel inside the passenger cabin in an approved soft-sided carrier placed beneath the seat. Current policy includes dogs and cats accepted; a maximum combined weight of **8 kg** on transatlantic flights (**5 kg** on regional routes); one pet per passenger; and pets must remain inside the carrier throughout the flight.

Hold transport: {{fait: airline_air_caraibes | hold_policy}}

Dogs exceeding cabin limits may travel inside the aircraft's ventilated and temperature-controlled hold. Current policy includes dogs and cats only; transport subject to aircraft quotas; an IATA-compliant rigid kennel required; and brachycephalic breeds prohibited in the hold on transatlantic flights.

Breed restrictions: {{fait: airline_air_caraibes | breed_restrictions}}

Certain snub-nosed (brachycephalic) breeds are prohibited from hold transport because of increased respiratory risks. French Category 1 dogs are not accepted. Category 2 dogs may be accepted subject to French legislation and additional documentation.

# Before booking

Before purchasing your ticket:

- verify destination-country veterinary requirements;
- reserve your dog's transport when booking;
- ensure your dog's microchip and vaccinations are valid;
- prepare an IATA-compliant kennel if travelling in the hold.

If travelling to or from the United States, additional CDC requirements also apply.

# Common mistakes

## Waiting too long before reserving your pet

Pet capacity is limited on every flight.

## Using a non-compliant kennel

Hold transport requires an IATA-approved rigid kennel.

## Forgetting destination-country regulations

Airline approval never replaces veterinary or customs requirements.

## Travelling with a brachycephalic breed

Many snub-nosed breeds cannot travel in the hold on transatlantic flights.

# Expert advice

Air Caraïbes is one of the best choices for travelling with a dog between mainland France and the French Caribbean. Because several Caribbean destinations have specific veterinary import requirements, begin preparing your paperwork several weeks before departure and reserve your dog's transport as early as possible.
