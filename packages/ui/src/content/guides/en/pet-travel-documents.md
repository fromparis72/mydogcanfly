There are two separate authorities in any international pet journey, and confusing them is the most expensive mistake in this field. **The airline decides how your pet flies.** **The destination decides whether it may enter.** They publish different requirements, on different schedules, and satisfying one says nothing about the other.

This guide covers the second one: the documents, what each is for, and why the order in which you obtain them is not negotiable.

## The four documents that recur

Requirements differ by destination, but almost all of them are assembled from the same four pieces.

- **The microchip.** A readable identifier under a recognised standard. It is the anchor for everything else: a vaccination that cannot be tied to a specific animal is not evidence of anything.
- **The rabies vaccination.** Administered *after* chipping, and recognised only after a waiting period set by the destination.
- **A health certificate**, issued by a veterinarian — often an officially approved one — within a short window before travel.
- **A rabies antibody titre test**, for destinations that require proof the vaccination actually worked. This is the step with the longest lead time.

Some destinations add treatments — tapeworm treatment is a common one — within a defined window before arrival. Our guide to [vaccinations and parasite treatment before a trip](/travel-hub/dog-travel-vaccinations/) covers the veterinary side.

## Why the order is not negotiable

Each document depends on the one before it, and several carry a mandatory delay:

1. **Chip first.** Always.
2. **Vaccinate after chipping.** A vaccination given to an unchipped animal is generally not recognised.
3. **Wait.** The vaccination becomes valid only after the destination's delay.
4. **Titre test where required**, taken a set time after vaccination — and frequently followed by a further waiting period of months before entry is permitted.
5. **Health certificate last**, inside its short validity window.

Get step 1 and 2 in the wrong order and you do not lose a day — you lose the vaccination, and every waiting period attached to it. This is why we recommend working backwards from the travel date rather than forwards from today: our [pet travel timeline](/tools/timeline/) does exactly that, and [planning an international pet flight backwards](/travel-hub/pet-flight-timeline/) explains the reasoning.

## The destination decides — so check the destination

Requirements vary enormously. Some destinations admit a vaccinated, chipped animal with a certificate. Others require a titre test and a long wait. A few operate quarantine. Island territories and rabies-free countries are typically the strictest.

Our [country guides](/countries/) set out the requirements destination by destination — more than a hundred and forty of them — and are the right starting point before you book anything. The broader picture is in [international travel with a dog](/travel-hub/international-travel-with-a-dog/).

Two details that catch people out:

- **Transit counts.** Some countries impose requirements on animals that merely pass through, which can make a connection harder than the destination itself. See [layovers and connections with a pet](/travel-hub/layovers-with-a-pet/).
- **Returning home is a journey too.** The rules for coming back are set by your own country and are not automatically satisfied by the outbound paperwork.

## What the airline checks, and what it does not

At check-in, staff verify that your documents match the destination's requirements — they are liable if they carry an animal that is refused entry. They will not tell you months in advance which documents to obtain, and they are not the authority on it.

What the airline *does* decide is the flying itself: crate compliance, breed acceptance, temperature limits, quotas. That side is covered in [how to book a flight with your dog](/travel-hub/booking-a-pet-flight/) and compared across carriers on our [airline pet policy pages](/airlines/).

## Keep the file together

Assemble one folder — physical, because that is what gets inspected — containing the chip number, the vaccination record, the certificate, the titre result where applicable, and your booking confirmation showing the animal. Our [travel sheet tool](/tools/fiche/) produces a single summary document you can put on top of it.

Carry it in hand luggage. A health certificate in a checked bag is, for the purposes of a check-in desk, a health certificate you do not have.
