Travellers reasonably assume an airline's pet policy is a settled document. It is not. It is a set of conditions that moves during the year, sometimes during the same month, and the version that binds you is the one in force **on your travel date, on your route, on the aircraft actually flying it**. This is the single most under-appreciated fact in pet air travel, and it is the reason a booking confirmed in March can fail in July.

## What actually changes, and why

Four things move, and they move for different reasons.

- **Temperature rules.** Most carriers suspend hold transport above or below certain temperatures, measured at departure, arrival and any stopover. These embargoes come and go with the season and are covered in our guide to [summer heat embargoes](/travel-hub/pet-heat-embargoes/).
- **Aircraft.** Hold access depends on a pressurised, temperature-controlled compartment. Swap the aircraft type and that compartment may be smaller, differently configured, or absent.
- **Route and destination.** The arrival country's import rules bear on what the airline will accept, which is why the same carrier can be permissive on one route and restrictive on another.
- **Breed rules.** Restrictions on snub-nosed and on certain other breeds are revised regularly, usually in the direction of more caution. See [snub-nosed breeds and flying](/travel-hub/snub-nosed-breeds-and-flying/).

None of these is arbitrary. Each is a safety or regulatory constraint that the airline does not fully control either.

## Why summaries go stale faster than you think

Any third-party summary — including ours — is a snapshot. We maintain a page per carrier precisely because the detail matters and moves: our [airline pet policy comparison](/airlines/) covers more than a hundred airlines, and it is built to be re-read rather than read once.

But a comparison table is a starting point, not a substitute for the carrier's own page on the day. Use it to shortlist routes and rule out impossible ones, then verify the shortlist directly. The order saves a great deal of time: it is far quicker to eliminate ten carriers from a table than to call ten airlines.

## The four checks that catch almost everything

Before you consider a booking safe, confirm these on the airline's own material:

1. **The operating carrier.** On a codeshare, the aircraft operator's rules apply, not the ticket seller's.
2. **The travel mode you were sold.** Cabin, accompanied hold baggage and manifest cargo are three different contracts — see [cabin, excess baggage or manifest cargo](/travel-hub/cargo-vs-excess-baggage/).
3. **The dimensions and total weight** accepted for your animal, in writing. Our [IATA crate calculator](/tools/crate/) gives you the figure to check against.
4. **The seasonal restrictions** in force on your dates, at every airport on the itinerary — including the connection, which is often the binding constraint. See [layovers and connections with a pet](/travel-hub/layovers-with-a-pet/).

Then repeat check 4 a few days before you fly.

## When the policy changes after you book

It happens, and it is not always announced. If the operating carrier, the aircraft or the season changes, treat the booking as unverified until you have re-confirmed it. Practical responses, in order of preference: move to a flight where the conditions still hold; move the animal to a different travel mode on the same carrier; or, if the destination allows it, shift the dates outside the embargo window — our [heat risk by route and month](/tools/heat/) shows which months are workable.

If none of that is possible, it is better to discover it two weeks out than at the desk. That is the whole argument for re-checking.

## Build the trip so a change is survivable

A trip planned with no margin breaks on the first surprise. Two habits help more than any amount of research: book the animal first and confirm it in writing, as set out in [how to book a flight with your dog](/travel-hub/booking-a-pet-flight/); and start early enough that a rebooking is still possible, which is what our [pet travel timeline](/tools/timeline/) is for.

Finally, keep the destination's requirements separate in your mind from the airline's. They are decided by different authorities and change on different schedules — [pet travel documents by destination](/travel-hub/pet-travel-documents/) and our [country guides](/countries/) cover that side.
