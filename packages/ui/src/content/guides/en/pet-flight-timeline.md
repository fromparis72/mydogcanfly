Most guides to flying with a pet present a checklist. A checklist implies the items can be done in any order and in parallel, and for an international move that is exactly the wrong mental model. Several steps carry **mandatory waiting periods**, and those periods — not the paperwork, not the booking — decide whether a given departure date is possible at all.

So the useful method is backwards. Fix the date you want to travel, then work back through the constraints and see whether it survives.

## Start with the destination, not the airline

Before anything else, read what the arrival country requires. It determines the length of the whole process, and it varies from modest to prohibitive. Our [country guides](/countries/) cover more than a hundred and forty destinations, and [pet travel documents by destination](/travel-hub/pet-travel-documents/) explains what each document is for.

Two questions decide your timeline:

- **Is a rabies titre test required?** If yes, the process is measured in months, not weeks.
- **Is there a waiting period after the test?** Several destinations impose one, and it is long.

If the answer to both is no, a couple of months is usually comfortable. If yes, six months is a reasonable floor.

## Working backwards from departure

Read this from the bottom up — that is how to use it.

| When | What | Why it sits there |
|---|---|---|
| 6 months or more before | Microchip, then rabies vaccination, then titre test if required | Longest waiting periods; everything else depends on them |
| 3–4 months before | Book the flight and the pet spot | Cabin quotas are small and fill early |
| 2–3 months before | Buy and introduce the crate | Habituation takes weeks, not days |
| 1 month before | Confirm destination paperwork; book the vet appointment | Certificates have short validity — this is the appointment, not the certificate |
| 1–2 weeks before | Re-check the airline policy and the operating carrier | Policies and aircraft change |
| A few days before | Health certificate; final check of temperature restrictions | Certificate validity window; seasonal embargoes |
| The day | Documents in hand luggage, light early meal, arrive early | See the airport day guide |

Our [pet travel timeline tool](/tools/timeline/) builds this schedule for your own departure date, which is more useful than a generic table.

## The steps that cannot be compressed

Three of them, and they are worth stating plainly.

**The chip must precede the vaccination.** A rabies vaccination given to an unchipped animal is generally not recognised. Getting this wrong means revaccinating and restarting every delay attached to it.

**The vaccination waiting period is fixed.** It exists so that immunity has developed. No veterinarian, airline or agency can shorten it.

**The titre test has its own sequence.** Taken a defined time after vaccination, processed by an approved laboratory, and in several destinations followed by a further wait measured in months before entry is allowed.

Everything else — the crate, the booking, the carrier choice — can be rearranged. These cannot.

## What to do in parallel

While the waiting periods run, the rest of the preparation costs nothing but attention:

- **Book the flight and the pet spot.** Quotas are small; see [how to book a flight with your dog](/travel-hub/booking-a-pet-flight/) and compare carriers on our [airline pet policy pages](/airlines/).
- **Size and buy the crate**, then let the dog live with it. Use our [crate calculator](/tools/crate/) with the method in [measuring your dog for a crate](/travel-hub/measuring-your-dog-for-a-crate/), and give the habituation weeks — see [dog travel anxiety](/travel-hub/dog-travel-anxiety/).
- **Check the season.** A summer date on a hot route may be impossible in the hold whatever your paperwork says: our [heat risk tool](/tools/heat/) shows which months work, and [summer heat embargoes](/travel-hub/pet-heat-embargoes/) explains why.
- **Check the breed constraints early**, because they can rule out the hold entirely — see [snub-nosed breeds and flying](/travel-hub/snub-nosed-breeds-and-flying/).

## When the date does not survive the timeline

It happens, and it is much better to learn it in month one than in month five. The usual responses: move the departure, choose a destination with lighter requirements — our [destination finder](/tools/destinations/) is built for that — or travel by another mode, covered in [travelling by car](/travel-hub/traveling-by-car-with-a-dog/), [by train](/travel-hub/train-travel-with-a-dog/) and [by ferry](/travel-hub/ferry-travel-with-a-dog/).

What does not work is starting late and hoping for flexibility. There is none in the parts that matter.
