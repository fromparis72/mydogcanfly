Travellers tend to picture one decision — cabin or hold — with a single price attached. In reality there are **three distinct arrangements**, and they differ in almost everything: who you book with, how the price is built, what paperwork exists, who physically handles the animal, and where you hand it over.

Getting the vocabulary right matters, because asking for the wrong one wastes weeks. The overview of the three modes is in [flying with a dog: cabin, hold or cargo](/travel-hub/flying-with-a-dog-cabin-hold-cargo/); this guide looks at them as contracts.

## Cabin: a fee on your own booking

The animal travels in an approved carrier under the seat in front of you. Commercially this is the simplest: usually a flat fee added to your reservation, a quota of animals per flight, and a strict size and weight limit covering **dog plus carrier**.

What you gain is not just comfort. You can see the animal throughout, the temperature is regulated for people the whole time, and seasonal embargoes essentially do not apply — a point developed in [summer heat embargoes](/travel-hub/pet-heat-embargoes/).

The limiting factor is size, and it is decided by the space under the seat, which varies by aircraft. Measure for it specifically: see [the most comfortable carriers for small dogs](/travel-hub/small-dog-carriers/) and our [cabin carrier selection](/tools/best-carriers/).

## Accompanied hold baggage: your flight, the hold

Here the pet travels in the pressurised hold of **the aircraft you are on**, checked in at the passenger desk on your own booking. This is the arrangement most people mean by "in the hold".

Practical characteristics:

- Checked in at the passenger terminal, usually at a dedicated desk and earlier than normal baggage.
- Priced as a pet fee or an excess-baggage charge, depending on the carrier.
- Requires a rigid crate that meets the applicable requirements — see [choosing an airline-approved crate](/travel-hub/airline-approved-dog-crate/) and size it with our [crate calculator](/tools/crate/).
- Subject to temperature embargoes and to breed restrictions, notably for [snub-nosed breeds](/travel-hub/snub-nosed-breeds-and-flying/).

Not every carrier offers it on every route, and several have withdrawn it entirely. That is one of the things that moves: see [why airline pet policies change](/travel-hub/airline-pet-policy-changes/).

## Manifest cargo: a freight shipment

The animal is booked as **freight**, through the airline's cargo division or an agent, with its own documentation. It is not an option added to your ticket; it is a separate transaction, sometimes even a separate company within the group.

What changes in practice:

- **Where you go.** Cargo terminals are usually a different building from the passenger terminal, occasionally a drive away.
- **When.** Drop-off is earlier and collection later, with customs formalities on international shipments.
- **How the price is built.** Typically from chargeable weight — a function of actual weight and volume — rather than a flat fee. This is why crate size drives cargo pricing so strongly.
- **Who handles it.** Specialised live-animal handling, often with dedicated holding facilities.

Cargo is frequently the only route for a large dog, for restricted breeds where the carrier permits it that way, and for destinations that require it. It is not a downgrade — it is a different contract with different strengths.

## Comparing them honestly

| | Cabin | Accompanied hold baggage | Manifest cargo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booked with | Your reservation | Your reservation | Cargo division or agent |
| Where you hand over | You keep the animal | Passenger desk | Cargo terminal |
| Price built from | Flat fee | Fee or excess baggage | Weight and volume |
| Same flight as you | Yes | Yes | Not guaranteed |
| Seasonal embargoes | Essentially not | Yes | Yes, differently applied |
| Size ceiling | Under-seat space | Crate and hold limits | Highest |

## Choosing, in practice

Size and breed usually decide before price does. If the cabin is available to you, it is generally the best answer. If it is not, compare what the carriers on your route actually offer — our [airline pet policy pages](/airlines/) cover more than a hundred — and price the two hold options separately, because a large crate can invert the comparison.

Then verify the destination side, which is decided by another authority entirely: see [pet travel documents by destination](/travel-hub/pet-travel-documents/) and our [country guides](/countries/). And whichever contract you end up with, book the animal before the ticket — the reasoning is in [how to book a flight with your dog](/travel-hub/booking-a-pet-flight/).
