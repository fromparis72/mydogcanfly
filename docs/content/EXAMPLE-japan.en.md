## Overview

Japan is a rabies-free country and enforces one of the strictest dog-import processes in the world. [s1] The process is entirely feasible for a well-prepared traveller, but it rewards planning months ahead — not weeks. [s2] Everything below is procedural: dates and thresholds are pulled live from our verified database, shown as bracketed facts.

## Entry requirements

Your dog needs an ISO microchip implanted before its rabies vaccinations, a valid rabies vaccination, and a blood test (titer) proving immunity. [s1] After a successful titer, there is a mandatory waiting period of {{fait: country_jp | délai d'attente après le titrage antirabique}} before your dog may enter. [s1] You must also notify the Animal Quarantine Service in advance — at least {{fait: country_jp | préavis à l'Animal Quarantine Service}} before arrival. [s2]

## Step by step

First, microchip the dog. [s1] Second, vaccinate against rabies at least twice after the chip. [s1] Third, run the titer test at an approved laboratory and start the waiting clock. [s1] Fourth, file the advance import notification and book a flight into an airport with an Animal Quarantine counter. [s2] Inspection on arrival is short when the paperwork is complete. [s2]

## Common mistakes

The single most common failure is vaccinating before microchipping — Japan will not count a rabies shot given before the chip. [s1] The second is underestimating the titer waiting period and booking the flight too early. [s1]

## FAQ

**Is there quarantine on arrival?** If every requirement is met and documented, the on-arrival inspection is brief and quarantine is minimal; gaps in the paperwork can trigger detention. [s1]

**Which airports can I fly into?** Only airports with an Animal Quarantine Service counter accept animal imports — confirm before booking. [s2]

**How far ahead should I start?** Because of the titer waiting period, most owners begin the process well over half a year before departure. [s1]
